About Me

I’m Rushil, a fourth-year student at Northeastern in Boston. Outside of class, I’m into fitness, music, travel, and soccer. I recently got into bouldering, it’s a great workout, but what I really like is the problem-solving side of it. My music taste jumps around a lot, from hip-hop to funk and soul to jazz, and my vinyl collection started with Malibu by Anderson .Paak. I love traveling and trying food from around the world, and most recently I got scuba certified in Mexico. I’m also a huge FC Barcelona fan and have watched every game since middle school. At Northeastern, I study Finance and Economics with a minor in Data Science, and I’ve done co-ops in investment management and equity research. What I like most about finance is figuring out why markets move the way they do, which is what this site is about.

Specifically, this site looks at one question: does the party in power matter for the stock market? Every election cycle, people on both sides claim the market loves or hates whoever just won, and this week’s midterms were no different. After Democrats took control of Congress, stocks swung up and down for days. I wanted to look at the actual numbers. The Markets page compares the S&P 500’s yearly returns under Democratic and Republican presidents going back to 1993, and the results are less clear-cut than either side would like.
